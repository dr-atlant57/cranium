Neck always tense? Start with what we can actually check.

CRANIO combines percussion-assisted work with the muscles of the upper neck and occipital region with observations of comfort and movement before and after a session. It is more than an abstract concept or a tracking journal.

A practical explanation

What to expect at a first visit

We start with conversation and observation, then practical work if there is no reason to recommend medical assessment or to avoid the session.

01 · Conversation

What you notice, how long it has been present, relevant assessments and warning signs.

02 · Initial observations

Comfort, movement and — with consent — standardized photographs, when appropriate.

03 · Practical session

A practitioner applies percussion-assisted soft-tissue work to the upper neck and occipital muscles, adjusting intensity to the individual.

04 · Reassessment

We compare observations, including when nothing changes, and consider whether further visits make sense.

Observations, not promises

What we pay attention to

The practitioner has reported individual changes in perceived neck tension, freedom of movement and visible symmetry. These reports do not establish treatment efficacy.

Movement

Turning and tilting the head; any limitation and sense of effort.

Experience

Perceived tension, head comfort and how you feel before and after.

Symmetry

Comparable photos, with informed consent, consistent angle and lighting.

How case records are documented ↗

The C0–C1 model

Why the occipital region matters in this approach

C0 refers to the occipital bone, and C1 to the first cervical vertebra (atlas). CRANIO explores a hypothesis that relative skull position and muscular adaptation in this region could contribute to some movement and postural compensation patterns.

This is a research hypothesis, not an established explanation for all asymmetry, headaches or other conditions. External observation cannot establish bone displacement.

Who is behind CRANIO

CRANIO SYSTEMS is an author-developed practical approach and research model by Lian Axis, informed by years of personal observations of head and neck work. Describing this experience is not a clinical efficacy claim.

Read about the method ↗

When to seek medical care first

Sudden severe headache, numbness or weakness, speech or vision changes, or pain after a head or neck injury require urgent medical assessment. Do not substitute a nonmedical session for necessary care.

CRANIO is a nonmedical soft-tissue practice and self-observation framework, not diagnosis or treatment.

Quick answers

Do I need to buy a device?

No. The practitioner uses the percussion tool during a session.

Will my atlas be adjusted?

No cervical manipulation or vertebral adjustment is claimed. Work focuses on soft tissue, and external observation alone cannot verify bone alignment.

Will one session fix the problem?

No results are guaranteed. We document what changes and what does not. Discuss medical symptoms with a clinician.

What is TwinMind?

An optional concept for continuity of observation records. No app or account is required for a session.
