When life stops feeling rewarding

Work, money, family, achievements: from the outside, life may look good. But the things that once excited you no longer feel the same. Even time off may not bring back a sense of ease.

When your thoughts go round in circles

In the practitioner's experience, some of the most striking changes were observed among people describing anxiety, panic attacks, constant worry and intrusive replaying of distressing experiences. After an emotional shock, the same thought can return again and again, like a record stuck on repeat.

When other support is needed

If you have lost your sense of meaning, experience persistent loss of pleasure or think about death, speak to a mental health professional. In immediate danger, contact local emergency services and someone you trust. The paid programme is not for people in crisis.

SOMA PARADISO · EXPERIENCES

Stories of life changes

CASE 001

The actor who lost his reason to live

A theatre actor was facing a severe crisis, suicidal thoughts and episodes of heavy drinking. A concerned friend helped him seek support.

After two visits involving correction and a reset session, people close to him noticed changes within 7–10 days. Two to three months later, his friend reported that he was directing, pursuing business and building relationships.

When the past would not let go

A woman had experienced violence during adolescence. She wanted to marry and start a family, yet intrusive memories and fears kept returning. She was afraid that her marriage would not work out.

Five reset sessions. According to the practitioner, after five sessions the thoughts that had troubled her stopped recurring. She later married and, as last reported to the practitioner, is happy in her marriage.

One person's experience does not predict everyone's outcome.

CASE 003

From recurring anxiety to confident public speaking

A man described anxiety and panic attacks, periodic IV treatments and psychiatric hospital stays, family difficulties and a stalled business. He sought help after a prolonged period of despair.

Previously, he had IV treatments about every three months and psychiatric inpatient care about every six months; he also took prescribed medication and saw a psychologist.

The practitioner reports a course of two main sessions and four reset sessions.

Following a course of sessions, the practitioner reports that his family relationships improved, business grew roughly threefold and his public speaking became calm and confident. He also began offering psychology consultations.

CASE 004

A millionaire who began to feel like a multimillionaire

A financially successful person knew he was a millionaire, but felt his inner sense of possibility did not match his achievements.

After a full course he described a new sense of depth, breadth and possibility, saying he now 'felt like a multimillionaire'. This describes a subjective experience, not verified growth in wealth.

Accounts are based on the practitioner's and witnesses' recollections, not proof of causation or guaranteed outcomes.
